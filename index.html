Talk Fancy: Turn Plain Words Into Grand Ones

Fancy English is plain English dressed for a formal room. It swaps short everyday words for longer Latinate ones, drops contractions, and keeps the tone calm and polite. Try it with the free fancy English translator when you want a full version, or play with the small taster below first.

You do not need a large vocabulary to sound polished. A few steady swaps do most of the work.

Ask becomes inquire. Buy becomes purchase. Get becomes obtain. Once you learn a handful of these pairs, formal writing starts to feel natural.

When fancy English helps

Formal words suit job letters, polite requests, and speeches. They signal care and respect for the reader. They also slow a reader down in a good way, since each word carries a little more weight.

Fancy words suit some moments and not others. A text to a close friend can sound odd in grand words. A note to a hiring manager or a teacher can gain from them. Match the words to the moment and the reader.

Worked examples

The five examples below come straight from the tool above. Each plain sentence went through the same word list you just used. No example was edited by hand after the tool ran.

Plain: I want to buy a new house.

Fancy: I desire to purchase a novel residence.

Plain: She wants to talk with her old friend.

Fancy: She desires to converse with her venerable companion.

Plain: I can't get good food.

Fancy: I cannot obtain excellent fare.

Plain: We will show the new house and buy good food.

Fancy: We will demonstrate the novel residence and purchase excellent fare.

Plain: Start the new job now.

Fancy: Commence the novel vocation now.
